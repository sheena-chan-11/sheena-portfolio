Driving Traffic to Pet Care Resources Through Homepage Redesign

Pro Bono Project

Small Angel Rescue, Inc

My Role

UI/UX Designer, WordPress Designer

My deliverables

User Research, Persona, Competitive Analysis, Sitemap, Wireframe, UI Design, Implemented the Design on WordPress

Duration

4 months

Scroll Down

Background

Small Angel Rescue is a nonprofit animal rescue in the Maryland area. They work with local shelters to rescue, rehome, and rehabilitate small animals, including hamsters, rats, guinea pigs, gerbils, and mice. Their goal is to build a sustainable, reputable, strategic, and ethical community for small animals. I worked with them to educate pet owners about proper animal care.

Process

My initial impression

The original site’s color and layout felt different from other animal-rescue platforms. Visitors could miss that the organization rescues small animals unless they read closely. The navigation made key information hard to find, and the dated design could discourage people from donating or helping.

Quantitative and qualitative research

In my previous job, I used GA to pull site reports. I thought I could do the same on Small Angel Rescue to monitor their user behavior, so I set that up on their site and started collecting and analyzing users' data.

I’ve also interviewed the volunteers, sent a survey to stakeholders and newsletter subscribers to learn about their feelings and improvements that they wish to have on the site.

After receiving all the responses, I gathered and analyzed the data through affinity mapping and listed out the summary in an infographic.

The responses gave me background details of our users, and I created Marissa to represent the user group.

What users needed:
  • The site wasn’t mobile-friendly, even though most visitors used mobile.
  • The dated design made users less inclined to stay.
  • Long, text-heavy pages discouraged people from reading.
Exploring solutions

As I was new to the animal rescue platform, I conducted a competitive analysis to grow my basic understanding of an animal rescue platform.

After I had some basic understanding of the animal rescue platforms, I brainstormed different potential solutions to address the user pain points, so I created a "How Might We" statement to explore all the possibilities.

How might we make it easier for first-time pet owners to find pet care information when they land on Small Angel Rescue's homepage?

  • Restructure and relocate information.
  • Use fewer words and more imagery to explain key topics.
  • Make high-demand information easy to spot.
  • Use multiple calls to action that lead directly to important pages.
  • Give high-demand information dedicated space on the homepage.
Restructuring and testing the experience

I worked with stakeholders to regroup the site’s content into a new sitemap and rename the navigation labels so visitors could better predict what each section contained.

Google Analytics showed that more than 68% of visitors browsed on mobile, so I used a mobile-first approach and developed four homepage directions. I shared them with volunteers in usability tests and asked them to:

  • Find the pet-care pages and learn how to care for the animals.
  • Find the adoption information.
  • Share how the landing page felt to navigate.

Volunteers described the homepage as clean and easy to use. They felt the imagery helped them connect with the organization, and having popular information on the homepage made it easier to find.

Implementation

After usability testing, I consolidated the feedback and finalized a UI that stayed within Small Angel Rescue’s brand colors. Guinea pigs were the most popular pets in the research, so I chose one for the homepage hero to quickly signal the organization’s focus.

The site was managed by stakeholders who were unfamiliar with WordPress. I taught myself the platform through tutorials, online sessions, and forums, then used custom CSS to work around WordPress limitations and build the interface we wanted.

Impact

One month after launch, Google Analytics showed that pet-care page views had doubled and engagement time was 5% longer. Visitors were spending more time with the care information, supporting the organization’s goal of helping people care for and protect small animals.

💭 Reflection
  • This was the first project where I launched my design, and I didn’t account for technical constraints early enough. Stakeholders and I liked the transparent desktop navigation, but it was challenging to reproduce in WordPress, so I had to leave it out on tablet and mobile.
  • My online-campaign experience was useful in this pro bono project. Combining analytics with interviews and a survey made the research richer—and showed that almost 70% of visitors used mobile.
